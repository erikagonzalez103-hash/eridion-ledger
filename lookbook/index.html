Luxury Custom Home · Colorback Glass

The tile look, without the grout

Primary Suite Wet Room — New Luxury Build, DFW Metroplex
Scope
Wet room walls, primary suite renovation
Systems
Tempered colorback glass, digitally color-matched to the tile spec
Client
Luxury builder partner + design team
Timeline
1 week, final measure to install

ChallengeThe designer had fallen in love with a specific tile aesthetic for the wet room walls — but the homeowner, experienced with luxury home maintenance, was adamant about not living with grout. Porous grout joints harbor mold and mildew in wet environments and never stop demanding upkeep.

ApproachRather than abandon the design, we fabricated tempered colorback glass panels digitally color-matched to the exact tile shade — the aesthetic the designer loved, in a non-porous surface that meets Glass Association of North America performance standards. Installation was single-stage and fit the construction schedule without tile's multi-step process.

ResultThe finished wet room delivers the designer's vision with none of the maintenance: non-porous glass requires 60–70% less cleaning time and chemicals than tile-and-grout systems, and there's no grout to crack, discolor, or generate builder callbacks. The completed bathroom was later featured in print editorials.

“I will not deal with grout in my shower.”

The homeowner's brief — honored in full
Designer's Personal Home · Mirrors

Grey mirror in a designer's own home

Entry Powder Bath — Southlake, TX
Scope
60″ × 84″ grey mirror — counter toward an 11-ft ceiling
Systems
1/4″ grey mirror · precision cutouts for the wall-mounted faucet
Client
Interior designer — her own home, the first of two
Timeline
Sequenced over several weeks with the full sub team

ChallengeThis was the first home we did for an interior designer — her own place in Southlake, about 10,000 square feet. The entry powder bath mattered to her because she likes to entertain, and that's the room guests actually see. She wanted it to make a statement, and she wanted the mirror to be different — but she was also planning artwork for the room, and we didn't want the mirror taking too much away from that focal point.

ApproachGrey mirror got it done on both counts: different enough to notice, quiet enough that the art stays the focus. One panel, roughly 60 by 84 inches at a quarter inch thick, running from the countertop up toward the eleven-foot ceiling, with cutouts for the wall-mounted faucet. Cutouts make any piece of glass more fragile, especially at a quarter inch — these sat far enough from the edges to keep the piece manageable, and Dion is meticulous about his measurements. Years of measuring colorback glass will do that. Fit wasn't a problem.

ResultThe mirror went in as part of the larger build — a lot of moving parts, several weeks, a layered team effort by all the subs. The room came out the way she wanted: the mirror gets noticed, the art keeps the room. And she hired us again for her family's next home before they moved out of state.

“Designers see everyone's work up close. Getting picked for her own home told us where we stand.”

Owner, Eridion Glass
Custom Residential · Shower Enclosures

Privacy that didn't look like a compromise

Boys' Bath — 5-Bedroom New Build, DFW Suburb
Scope
72″ × 90″ opening — 2-panel inline, 28″ pivot door
Systems
3/8″ grey-tinted tempered · dual-swing pivot · matte black hardware
Client
Builder partner
Timeline
1 week, measure to install · 2-day set

ChallengeIn a five-bedroom new build, the boys' rooms shared a bathroom — no ensuite in the plan. The teenage son wanted more privacy than a clear enclosure gives, and his mom brought the request to the build team looking for recommendations, not products.

ApproachWe recommended grey-tinted glass over the usual satin-etch. Same privacy, different message: frosted glass reads as a utility fix, while grey reads as a design decision — edgier, more modern, and right at home with the house's matte black finishes. Fabricated as a two-panel inline enclosure in 3/8-inch tempered grey glass with a 28-inch pivot door that swings both in and out.

ResultSet on the standard one-week measure-to-install timeline, about two days on site. The family loved it — and that word came back through the builder, which counts double.

“Builders don't usually call about a shower. When she called to say the family loved it — that's a big deal.”

Owner, Eridion Glass
Custom Residential · Glass Railings

A staircase that looks like it's floating

Two-Story Foyer — Large Custom Home, DFW
Scope
241.5 sq ft — 15 panels, stair flights + both loft runs
Systems
1/2″ tempered Starphire (low-iron) · adjustable standoffs
Client
Stair contractor partner — builds large custom homes
Timeline
Templated per panel · set in about a day, two-man crew

ChallengeThe ask on this one was simple to say and hard to do: make the staircase look like it's floating. Floating oak treads in a two-story foyer, glass climbing the stair and wrapping both sides of the loft above it — and the glass had to disappear. We came in through a stair contractor new to us who builds massive homes. We weren't there at the drawing stage, but on site, while the build gets finalized, we have a lot of input.

ApproachStarphire glass is what makes it work: half-inch tempered, low-iron — standard glass reads green at that thickness, and Starphire is almost invisible. You can tell. Fifteen panels, 241.5 square feet, a little over half a ton of glass. Every panel gets measured individually, and a template made and numbered so there's no confusion. We learned that lesson a long time ago — templates are a small expense in the long run, and worth every penny.

ResultTwo men set it in about a day. The templates and adjustable standoffs kept the hours down — measure twice — and that kind of install speed is another reason builders choose Eridion. The staircase floats, the glass disappears, and the stair contractor has already slotted us for their next two homes.

“The ask was to make the staircase float. I think we read the assignment.”

Owner, Eridion Glass
Builder's Parents' New Build · Colorback Glass

The kitchen everyone ends up in

Family Kitchen — New Build, Little Rock, AR
Scope
Full backsplash run — panels up to 12 ft, seams hidden at the vent hood
Systems
Colorback glass — exact match to the island's accent color
Client
Builder partner, multi-year Top Builder — her parents' home
Timeline
1 week from final measure

ChallengeThis one was for our builder partner's own parents. She's a Little Rock builder who's been named Top Builder there multiple years running — and this was her mom and dad's new build, so we knew we had to kick it up a notch. They're retired, and they weren't exactly sizing down. It's a big family that always congregates at their house: lots of kids, lots of grandkids, everyone cooking, everyone serving themselves around an oversized island. With that much traffic, grout behind the counters was going to be a losing battle. And this couple likes color — being able to choose their color mattered.

ApproachThey chose a dark green with blue undertones — a dual-tone color that can lean blue or lean green depending on the light — matched exactly to the accent color on the base of their island, so the backsplash and the island tie the room together. Measuring for these is an art form: custom cabinets in luxury homes mean you're working down to the millimeter, and installing in a finished house — freshly painted walls, freshly painted cabinets, brand-new appliances — means a tight fit with nothing you can afford to scratch. Glass panels run up to about twelve feet, so on a long counter you have to be crafty about the seams. Here, they sit at the cabinet lines flanking the vent hood: the hood holds your eye, the symmetry does the rest, and you just don't see them. We use that one a lot. Cutouts are their own discipline — every outlet, every notch under the cabinets — and where the color allows it, we back-paint glass outlet and switch covers to match exactly, so even the switches disappear into the run.

ResultThe family was thrilled — and then they asked the question everyone asks when we present colorback glass: why don't more people choose this? Why don't people know about it? Since people always want to know what it costs, the honest answer: more than tile, less than marble or granite. The difference lives in the cutouts, the precision fit, and a paint made to stand up to heat and cold. You get what you pay for.
